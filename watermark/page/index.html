Harmonic phase watermark

Data riding inside the voice itself, so it passes through a repeater the way the speech does. The sender locks to the talker's pitch and turns the phase of the odd harmonics between 500 and 2600 Hz so each 32 ms slot carries four bits. Here it carries a repeating ID frame: callsign AG7EW and a one-byte "tone" code, protected by a CRC.

Listen first: does B sound different from A? Toggle while it plays; both stay in sync. Headphones help.

Does the ID get through?

Each channel was run on both voices. Raw bits are slot bits the receiver read where the sender had marked, with the share it got wrong. ID decoded is how many seconds of talking passed before the receiver decoded the full frame (and confirmed it on the next try). Unmarked speech was decoded through every channel as a control.

How it works

  1. sotto's pitch tracker follows the talker's fundamental, F0. The sender tracks the 300-3000 Hz version of its own speech, which is what a receiver will hear, and marks only where that agrees with the full-band pitch.
  2. Each harmonic k is mixed down by k·F0 and low-passed at 30 Hz, giving its slowly varying amplitude and phase.
  3. For an odd harmonic and its even neighbours, the second phase difference ignores timing, delay and small pitch errors entirely. The sender rotates the odd harmonic by at most 90° to set it to 0° (bit 0) or 180° (bit 1). The even harmonics stay untouched as the reference.
  4. The receiver tracks pitch on what it hears, reads the same quantity, finds slot timing and the repeater's filter phase bend blindly, and feeds soft bits to a K=7 rate-1/2 Viterbi decoder.

Dk = arg( zk+1 · zk−1 / zk² ) = ψk+1 − 2ψk + ψk−1  →  0° or 180°

What it can and can't do yet

  • Nothing gets through in pauses or unvoiced sounds: only about 40% of slots carry data, so the four bits per slot work out to roughly 20-50 usable raw bits per second while someone talks.
  • The rotation has to be the full amount. At 60% strength the speech's own phase pattern leaks through and a repeater pushes bit errors past 15%; at 40% even a clean wire gives 25% errors.
  • The receiver is offline: whole-utterance pitch tracking and zero-phase filters. A live receiver needs a causal tracker and about 50 ms of delay.
  • Everything here is simulated. An over-the-air test through the Pluto on 70 cm is the next step.
