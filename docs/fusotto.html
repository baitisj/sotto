Digital voice on amateur radio has a locked room in the middle of it. The air interface is published, and the voice codec inside it is not. FuSotto keeps the standard frame, so radios still sync and still show who is calling, and puts an open codec where the closed one used to be. It runs on handhelds people already own, with no hardware changes.

The locked room

Yaesu System Fusion and DMR are 4800-baud four-level FSK modes, and their frame layouts are public. The voice inside them is not. Fusion carries speech with DVSI's AMBE+2 vocoder in its DN mode and IMBE in Voice FR mode, and DMR uses AMBE+2. To make or play that voice you need DVSI's chip or its licensed software. Anyone can build a radio that frames a Fusion call correctly; the voice is the part nobody can open, study or improve.

M17 showed the other way: an open protocol with the open Codec 2 vocoder. But M17 started from scratch, and most of the digital radios on the air today speak C4FM or DMR.

FuSotto takes the frames everyone already has and opens the part that was locked. The sync words, the frame headers and the callsign fields stay exactly as the standards define them. Only the voice payload changes, to sotto, an open low-rate voice codec (MIT licence) built on Codec 2's analysis and synthesis.

Three names, one idea

sotto in C4FM framing

FuSotto

A standard 100 ms C4FM frame: sync, FICH, then 720 payload bits. Each frame carries five 48-bit sotto frames, each with its own CRC-8, under a tail-biting K=7 convolutional code at rate 7/18, spread across the frame and scrambled. The header and terminator carry the callsigns in the usual place.

sotto in DMR framing

DSotto

An ordinary DMR group voice call. The 216 voice bits of each burst (one every 60 ms on a timeslot), which normally hold three AMBE+2 frames, hold sotto blocks under our own rate 1/2 code instead: 9 bytes of sotto a burst, 1200 bit/s in real time.

sotto in M17 framing

M17-sotto

An M17 stream whose link setup marks the payload as data and tags it SOTTO 1200. Each 40 ms frame carries two sotto superframes and a CRC-16. M17's own voice is already open; M17-sotto adds sotto's lower rates and the same payload the other two modes carry.

What we stand for

Keep the air interface

A good frame format is shared infrastructure. We don't invent a new modulation or a new sync word. A stock radio should see a normal call from a known station.

Open all the way down

Codec, channel code, framers and test tools are published. Each C framer is tested byte for byte against a Python reference that anyone can read.

The radios you already own

No solder, no hardware mods. Everything below runs on unmodified handhelds through firmware, RAM modules and the programming cable.

Identify, always

Every test transmission sends the station's callsign: in Morse before each key-up and in every frame header.

Measure before claiming

Each result on this page comes from a bench log with frame counts and CRCs. Where something is untested or broken, we say so.

Nothing borrowed that isn't ours

No manufacturer firmware is used, changed or redistributed. Frame layouts come from public descriptions and open software, and every source is credited.

Where it stands

Three radios, three modes. "Sends" and "hears" mean whole sotto messages, decoded with every CRC checked. The SDR is an ADALM-Pluto running our open softmodem.

Not done yet

  • Stock Fusion and DMR radios can't play sotto. Only radios with open firmware, or an SDR, can. We haven't yet checked how a stock Fusion radio reacts to FuSotto voice frames: whether it shows the call, stays quiet, or plays noise.
  • MMDVM hotspots and repeaters rebuild the voice FEC of every frame they relay, which would scramble a sotto payload. FuSotto won't cross those networks until MMDVMHost learns to leave flagged frames alone.
  • The FICH bit that marks a frame as sotto is provisional.
  • DMR receive on the UV-380 doesn't work yet, so DSotto has only been decoded by the SDR.
  • The UV-K6 receives C4FM frames in emulation, byte for byte, and is now on the bench. It can't play sotto yet.
  • The UV-380 records a message and then sends it, with a 5 s key-up limit (about 8 s of sotto). Talking live while keyed comes later.
  • Every test so far has been on the bench. Range and mobile tests come later.

Take part

The codec, the softmodem and the radio work are all public. The softmodem sends and receives FuSotto through a radio's 9600-baud data port and a sound card, or through an SDR, so you can listen without new hardware.
