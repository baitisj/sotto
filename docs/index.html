sotto

Open 2400 bit/s digital voice for FM radios: a vocoder, error correction and a C4FM modem path, built for small fixed-point DSPs.

Part 1: same encoder, different channel protection

sotto's own encoder isn't built yet, so every clip here uses the stock Codec 2 2400 encoder. Each went through System Fusion (C4FM) frames, a simulated mobile radio channel with fading and noise, and our software receiver. The rows differ only in how the voice bits are protected and where they sit in the frame. Encoder-against-encoder comparisons (sotto's encoder next to Codec 2's, through the same channel and receiver) come next.

UHF 446 MHz, 60 km/h, 52 dB-Hz (a weak mobile signal) same transmit power for every row

The score is PESQ, an automatic speech-quality estimate from 1 (bad) to 4.5 (excellent), averaged over three fading runs. "No radio channel" is the best any row can reach with today's codec.

Slow VHF fading: the hard case

At 146 MHz and town-driving speed (30 km/h), fades last longer than the 100 ms interleaver, so whole frames are lost. This is sotto at 56 dB-Hz. Longer interleaving and better concealment are on the list.

What you're hearing

sotto (VW slot) puts the voice in the full-rate part of the Fusion frame, adds a CRC to each 20 ms frame and protects everything with a strong tail-biting convolutional code. DN slot + our code uses the half-rate slot Fusion radios use for DN mode, with a weaker version of the same code. Fusion-style protection mimics how DN protects its voice bits (the first 27 bits sent three times, the rest unprotected), applied to Codec 2. That's a statement about Codec 2 with that protection, not about AMBE+2, whose decoder is built around it.

Today's vocoder is stock Codec 2 2400. The next step for the project is a better open vocoder at the same rate, which should lift every row. The female voice especially: Codec 2 itself costs her about 0.7 PESQ before any radio channel.
