sotto-tts

Type something and hear it spoken at 1,200 bit/s. Text becomes sotto 1200 superframes, 6 bytes per 40 ms, which the K6's fixed-point decoder turns into speech, all in your browser.

What runs here is C compiled to WebAssembly (–): English letter-to-sound rules, a small CMUdict lexicon, rules for timing and intonation (or, with Trained, duration and pitch trees learnt from Kokoro reading sentences, –), a unit table that stores voicing, energy and sotto codebook indices per 20 ms but never pitch, and the K6's fixed-point sotto 1200 coder and decoder. Callsigns are spoken in NATO phonetics.

The diphone table holds 1,634 sound pairs cut from the Kokoro af_sarah voice reading two hours of real sentences; the robot table (4.1 KB) holds four averaged frames per phone, with start and end frames picked by the neighbouring sounds. Callsign letters, digits and "stroke" are whole words (5 KB), each cut from Sarah reading it in a short spelled list. Code: c/tts, write-up: docs/sotto-tts.md, and a course on how it works: How sotto-tts talks.
