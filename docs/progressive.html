Progressive sotto

A sotto 2400 clip does not have to arrive in time order. Sent in scans, like a progressive JPEG, the receiver has a rough copy of the whole clip after a quarter of the bits, and it sharpens as the rest comes in. Squeeze the link with the resistor, pause it, and listen: the decoder plays whatever has arrived at each moment.

Same 48-bit frames as a streamed clip, only sent in another order, so a complete clip decodes to exactly the streamed audio. Design and scores: docs/sotto-progressive.md. Decoding runs here in the browser (the C decoder built to WebAssembly). Back to the main page.

What you are looking at

Each column of the grid is one 20 ms frame of the clip; each row is one of its fields: voicing (2 bits), pitch (7), energy (5) and the four stages of the spectral shape (9, 9, 8, 8). A cell lights up when all of its bits have arrived, and flashes as it lands. Below it is the spectrogram of what the decoder makes of the frames so far, redrawn as data arrives, and the original's spectrogram for comparison. The white line is the playhead; click the grid to move it.

  • Streamed fills the grid from left to right, one whole frame at a time. On a link slower than real time it can't start playing until most of the clip is in, or it runs dry.
  • Progressive (script SI) first sends voicing, pitch, energy and the first shape stage of every other frame, which is 24% of the bits and already intelligible, then the odd frames, then the finer shape stages. Inside each scan the frames go in a golden-ratio stride, so a lost packet takes frames spread all over the clip, and every gap has neighbours to fill from.
  • Just in time sends the first scan, then gives each frame the rest of its fields while they can still land before that frame plays, at the speed the resistor was set to when you pressed Start. What doesn't fit comes after, so the copy you keep ends up complete.

Missing fields are filled the way docs/sotto-progressive.md section 4 says: voicing, pitch and energy from the nearest frames that have them, the sum of whichever shape stages have arrived, a shape interpolated in time when a frame has none, and a prior shape per voicing class before any shape has arrived. The 4-8 kHz hiss band rides along: an unvoiced frame's hiss index is in its pitch bits.

The resistor sets the link's payload rate: 4.8 MΩ·bit/s divided by R, so 2 kΩ is real time for sotto 2400 (2400 bit/s). The K6 F/1800 preset is the K6's XL packets with their preambles (1.65 s of air per second of speech). Only sotto 2400 is here so far; sotto 1200 (where just in time plays at full quality from about 2 s) needs its fill rules in C first. Recordings are cut to 20 s.
